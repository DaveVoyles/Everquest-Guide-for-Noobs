# 🔨 Tradeskills in EverQuest

Tradeskills are EQ's crafting system — a deep, expensive, and often frustrating collection of professions that reward patient players with useful items, unique gear, and a profitable niche in the player economy. This guide gives you an overview of every tradeskill and helps you decide which (if any) to pursue.

---

## ⚠️ The Hard Truth About Tradeskills

EQ tradeskills are **not like crafting in modern MMOs**. There is no automatic recipe list. No "collect these 4 things and click craft." You must:

- **Find or buy recipes** (or look them up externally on Allakhazam/EQ wiki)
- **Gather or purchase components** — often expensive
- **Fail combines** — there's a significant chance to fail at low skill, consuming your materials
- **Invest time and plat** into raising the skill before it becomes useful

That said, a maxed tradeskill can be **extremely lucrative** and provides items you can't get any other way.

## 🔢 How Tradeskill Levels Work

Each tradeskill is its own skill, maxing at **300** (on EQLive; classic cap is lower). You raise it by successfully completing **combines** — putting ingredients into a container and hitting "Combine."

| Skill Level | Difficulty |
|------------|-----------|
| 0–50 | Easy — cheap trivial combines are available |
| 50–100 | Moderate cost — requires specific components |
| 100–150 | Gets expensive — failure rate is meaningful |
| 150–200 | Significant investment — better recipes unlock |
| 200–250 | Serious grind — endgame materials |
| 250–300 | Endgame only — very expensive, rare materials |

### Trivial Level
Every recipe has a **trivial level** — the skill number at which you can no longer fail the combine. Below trivial, you can fail and lose components. **Once your skill exceeds the trivial level for a recipe, it always succeeds.**

## 🧁 Baking

**Container:** Oven (found in most cities), Spit, or Portable Oven
**Components:** Flour, water, meat, spices — from NPC vendors and mob drops

Baking is the most approachable tradeskill. The basics (bread, simple food) are extremely cheap. At higher levels, baking produces:
- **Stat food** — food items that buff STR, STA, etc. while consumed. Very popular.
- **Combine reagents** for other tradeskills

**Worth it?** Yes for passive income. Stat food sells consistently in the Bazaar/EC tunnel.

### Baking Tip
Buy **Baking Supplies** from the provisioner vendor in any city. The basic recipe: Flour + Water + Leavening = Plain Bread. Almost zero cost, raises skill to ~50 quickly.

---

## 🍺 Brewing

**Container:** Brew Barrel (found in inns and taverns)
**Components:** Malted Barley, Hops, Malt, Water — vendor-bought and mob drops

Brewing produces:
- Alcohol (mostly flavor, some in-game effect)
- Some potions and buff items at higher levels
- Components used in other tradeskill recipes

**Worth it?** Niche. Cheap to raise skill, but the items it produces have limited demand except for specific components.

---

## 🧵 Tailoring

**Container:** Loom or Sewing Kit
**Components:** Pelts (from animals), silk (from spiders), patterns (vendor-bought), thread

Tailoring produces:
- **Leather and cloth armor** — useful for Druids, Enchanters, Wizards
- **Bags and containers** — one of the most valuable products
  - 8-slot, 10-slot, 12-slot backpacks are always in demand
  - A skilled tailor making high-capacity bags can earn significant plat
- **Spell-component pouches** and specialized containers

**Worth it?** Yes — especially for the bags. A tailor who can make **Backpacks** (8-slot) or better is always in demand. Materials (pelts, silk) are cheap and drop everywhere.

### Tailoring Tip
Farm **High Quality Pelts** from animals (bears, cats, etc.) — regular pelts are much less valuable. HQ pelts make better items and sell for more to other tailors.

---

## ⚒️ Smithing

**Container:** Forge (found in most cities)
**Components:** Metal bars (bronze, iron, steel), patterns, water flasks — expensive at higher tiers

Smithing produces:
- **Plate, chainmail armor** — for Warriors, Paladins, Shadow Knights
- **Weapons** — swords, axes, blunt weapons
- **Shields**

Smithing is the most expensive tradeskill to raise due to the cost of metal materials. However, high-level smiths can produce gear that competes with drops.

**Worth it?** Eventually, yes — but budget thousands of plat before committing. Not recommended for new players until they're financially stable.

---

## 💎 Jewelry Making

**Container:** Jeweler's Kit or Jewelry Mold
**Components:** Gems, precious metals (gold, silver, platinum bars), settings

Jewelry Making produces:
- **Rings and earrings** with stat bonuses (STR, STA, INT, WIS, etc.)
- **Pendants and amulets**
- Some items that serve as cultural armor components

**Worth it?** Yes — low-level stat jewelry is cheap to make and provides meaningful upgrades. Players always want rings with +STA or +INT.

---

## 🪵 Fletching

**Container:** Fletching Kit
**Components:** Arrow shafts, arrow points, feathers, strings

Fletching produces:
- **Arrows** and **bolts** for ranged combat
- **Bows** at higher skill levels

**Worth it?** Yes if you're a **Ranger or Bard** using ranged — making your own arrows is dramatically cheaper than buying them. Also, selling arrows to Rangers is a reliable small income stream.

---

## 🔬 Research (Spell Research)

**Container:** Research Kit (various types — different kits for different spell research)
**Components:** Spell pages, words, and components dropped from specific monsters

Research produces:
- **Spells** — specifically spells that cannot be bought from vendors
- Some of the **best spells in the game** for certain classes are research-only

**Classes that use it:** Wizards, Enchanters, Magicians, Necromancers primarily

**Worth it?** Absolutely for casters who want access to their full spell library. A skilled Researcher can also **sell researched spells** to other players for significant plat.

### Research Tip
Research components (spell words, pages) drop from monsters in many mid-to-high level zones. Keep them when you loot them — they sell well even if you don't do Research yourself.

---

## 🧪 Alchemy (Shaman Only)

**Container:** Mortar and Pestle
**Components:** Various herbs, reagents, monster parts — many are hard to find

Alchemy produces:
- **Potions** — buffs, heals, stat boosts, resistance potions
- Some potions available nowhere else in the game

**Shaman exclusive** — only Shamans can learn Alchemy.

**Worth it?** Yes for Shamans who are invested in the game. Potions provide flexibility for any class. However, raising Alchemy is expensive and the component sourcing is complex.

---

## 🐟 Fishing

**Container:** Fishing Pole + Bait (held in hands)
**Command:** `/fish`

Fishing produces:
- Various **fish** (mostly for Baking combines)
- Occasionally **rare items** (very rare)
- Some **tradeskill components**

**Worth it?** It's relaxing and nearly free to do. Don't expect to get rich, but it's a nice AFK activity while waiting for group or between sessions.

---

## 🚀 Getting Started: Recommended First Tradeskill

**For most new players:**

1. **Baking** — Cheapest to start, produces immediately useful items (food), good for learning the combine system
2. **Tailoring** — Learn it second; the bag-making potential is extremely practical
3. **Fletching** — If you're a Ranger, start this early for cheap arrows

**Avoid until you have significant plat:**
- Smithing (too expensive early)
- Alchemy (too complex early)
- Research (requires knowing where to farm components)

## 📚 Tradeskill Resources

You will need external resources to craft effectively — EQ's in-game recipe documentation is minimal:

- **Allakhazam** (allakhazam.com) — recipes, component lists, trivial levels
- **EQ Traders Corner** (eqtraders.com) — the definitive tradeskill reference site
- **P1999 Wiki** — for classic-era recipes
- **EQ Resource** (eqresource.com) — for EQLive

> 💡 **EQ Traders Corner is the gold standard.** It has guides, optimal leveling paths, and cost estimates for every tradeskill. Bookmark it before you start.
