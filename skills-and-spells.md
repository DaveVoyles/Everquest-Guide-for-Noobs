# 📚 Skills & Spells in EverQuest

Understanding how skills and spells work is fundamental to playing EQ well. Unlike modern MMOs, skills don't come from talent trees or level-ups — they improve through **use**, and spells must be **purchased, researched, or looted** and then **memorized**. This guide explains everything.

---

## 🛡️ Skills: How They Work

### Skills Improve by Using Them

EQ's skill system is straightforward: **use a skill, and it gets better**. If you're a Warrior who wants better 1H Slashing, fight with a 1H sword. If you want better Swimming, swim. The game rolls a chance to skill-up after each use.

You'll see this message when a skill increases:
> *"Your skill in 1H Slashing has increased."*

### Skill Caps Are Level-Dependent

Every skill has a **maximum value at your current level**. Even if you grind a skill constantly, it can't exceed your level's cap. As you level up, the cap rises and you can continue improving. The formula varies by class and skill, but generally:

- Skills cap around **200–252** at high levels (varies by skill and class)
- **Combat skills** cap much higher than utility skills
- Your **class** determines which skills you can train and how high they go

### Viewing Your Skills

Press **Alt+S** (or find it in your character window) to open the **Skills window**. You'll see:
- Current value of each skill
- The cap for your current level (if the skill bar isn't full, you have room to improve)

## 🎓 Training Points: Spent at Your Guild

When you create a character, you start with **5 training points** (the number varies by server/era). These are spent at your **class guild trainer** — an NPC in your starting city.

### What Training Points Buy

Training points let you **start** a skill at a higher level than 0, or unlock skills you don't automatically have. Common uses:

| Skill Type | Worth Training? | Notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| Primary weapon skill | ✅ Yes, priority | Start with your main weapon type at a higher level |
| Dodge / Parry / Riposte | ✅ Yes (melee) | Defense skills — very useful, slow to grind up |
| 1H Blunt / Slashing / Piercing | ✅ Yes | Whichever weapon type you'll use |
| Offense | ✅ Yes (melee) | Directly increases your to-hit chance |
| Baking / Brewing / other tradeskills | ⚠️ Optional | If you plan to craft early |
| Swimming | ❌ No | Improves quickly by just... swimming |
| Fishing | ❌ No | Low priority unless you want tradeskill mats |

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't spend training points on skills that level up quickly through normal play (like Swimming or Sense Heading). Save them for skills that are painful to grind from 0 — especially your primary weapon skill or defensive skills.

## ⚔️ When to Upgrade Skills

### Early Game (Levels 1–20)

- Spend your **training points immediately** at your guild trainer — don't sit on them
- Focus on your **primary weapon skill** and **defensive skills** (Dodge, Parry)
- Grind combat skills naturally — they'll improve during normal XP grinding

### Mid Game (Levels 20–45)

- Weapon skills should be near or at cap by now if you've been fighting actively
- Start paying attention to **Offense** (increases your chance to hit) and **Defense** (decreases chance to be hit)
- **Casters:** Focus on improving your **Channeling** skill — this reduces your chance of getting your cast interrupted when taking a hit
- **Monks:** Work on **Hand to Hand** or your preferred weapon type

### Late Game (Levels 45+)

- Most core combat skills should be near cap through normal play
- Consider spending AA (Alternate Advancement) points on passive skill improvements
- **Tradeskill players:** This is when serious tradeskill grinding begins — check the EQ wiki for the most efficient combine sequences

### Key Skills to Watch By Class

| Class | Priority Skills |
|-------|----------------|
| Warrior | 1H or 2H weapon, Defense, Offense, Dodge, Parry, Riposte, Taunt |
| Cleric | Channeling, 1H Blunt, Bash |
| Wizard | Channeling, Evocation, Alteration |
| Necromancer | Channeling, Conjuration, Alteration, Meditate |
| Enchanter | Channeling, Alteration, Abjuration, Meditate |
| Druid | Channeling, Alteration, Evocation, Meditate |
| Ranger | Archery, 1H or 2H weapon, Offense, Channeling |
| Rogue | Backstab, Sneak, Hide, 1H Piercing, Offense, Defense |
| Monk | Hand to Hand (or 2H Blunt), Kick, Flying Kick, Defense, Meditate |

| Shaman | Channeling, Alteration, Conjuration, Meditate |

| Paladin | Channeling, 1H Blunt, Defense, Lay Hands |

> 💡 **The most important skill for casters: Meditate.** A higher Meditate skill means faster mana regeneration while sitting. Level it early, level it often.

## 📖 Meditate: Casters' Most Important Skill

Casters sit down to regenerate mana, but the **Meditate skill** makes this dramatically more efficient.

- **Without Meditate:** Mana trickles back in while sitting
- **With Meditate (active):** Open your spellbook (press **B**) while sitting — this activates the Meditate skill and greatly increases mana regen rate
- You'll see: *"You are now meditating."*

**Level it by doing it constantly.** After every pull, sit and open your spellbook. You'll get skill-up messages regularly in your first 20 levels.

> 💡 There's a skill cap on Meditate per level. You can't outpace your level, but you should always be at or near cap — if your Meditate is below the level cap, you're leaving mana regen on the table.

## ✨ Spells: How They Work

Spells are separate from skills. **You must acquire, memorize, and manage your spells manually.**

### Spell Slots (Gems)

You have a limited number of **spell gem slots** — these are the spells you currently have memorized and can cast:

- **Classic/P1999:** 8 gem slots
- **EQLive (modern):** Up to 12 gem slots (varies by era)

You can only cast spells that are in a gem slot. To use a different spell, you must **memorize** it, which takes real time (seconds to a minute for high-level spells).

### Your Spell Book

Press **B** to open your spell book. This shows:
- All spells you own and can memorize
- Spells are organized by level within each level-group

To memorize a spell:
1. Open your spell book (**B**)
2. Drag a spell from the book to an empty gem slot
3. Wait for the casting bar to fill — spell is now memorized
4. The spell icon appears in your gem slot and is ready to cast

## 🛒 Where to Buy Spells

Spells are **purchased from spell vendors** in major cities, usually near your class guild. This is your primary source of new spells throughout the game.

### How to Find the Spell Vendor

- Look for NPCs with names like **"Fara the Spellseller"** or just ask in `/say` "Where is the spell vendor?"
- Hail the vendor → Click "Buy" → Browse by level
- You'll see spells you're eligible for based on your class and level

## 📅 When to Buy New Spells

### Buy Every Tier Promptly

New spells unlock at specific levels (usually every 4–5 levels). You should **buy new spells as soon as you reach the level** that unlocks them. Falling behind on spell upgrades is one of the most common mistakes casters make.

**Common spell tiers:**
- Levels 1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 29, 34, 39, 44, 49, 51, 53, 55, 57, 60...

### How Much Do Spells Cost?

Spells are priced in platinum (**pp**) and scale with level:

| Spell Level | Approximate Cost |
|------------|-----------------|
| 1–8 | 1–10 cp/sp (essentially free) |
| 9–16 | A few silver to 1 pp |
| 17–24 | 1–5 pp each |
| 25–34 | 5–20 pp each |
| 35–44 | 20–50 pp each |
| 45–54 | 50–200 pp each |
| 55–60 | 100–500+ pp each |

> 💡 **Budget for spells.** As a caster, don't blow all your plat on gear — you need it for spells. A new tier of spells can cost several hundred plat if you buy everything at once.

### Priority — Which Spells to Buy First

Don't buy everything at once. Prioritize:

1. **Your best direct damage nuke** (Wizards, Necromancers, Druids)
2. **Your main healing spell** (Clerics, Druids, Shamans)
3. **Your crowd control spell** (Enchanters: always buy latest Mez/Charm)
4. **Buffs for your group** — Spirit of the Wolf, haste, strength buffs
5. **Gate** (if your class gets it) — buy this ASAP at whatever level you unlock it
6. **Utility spells** — root, snare, slow — then worry about upgrading secondary ones

## 🔬 Other Ways to Get Spells

### Loot Drops
Some spells drop from monsters. These are typically:
- **Rare spells** not sold by vendors (Research components)
- **Powerful upgrades** for specific levels
- Worth a lot of plat if your class doesn't need them

### Research / Combining
Casters (especially Wizards and Enchanters) can **research spells** by combining components in a Research Kit. This unlocks spells that can't be purchased anywhere. The EQ wiki has detailed combine recipes per spell.

### Tradeskill-Researched Spells
Some of the best spells in the classic era are **research-only** — meaning you either:
1. Research them yourself (requires tradeskill investment)
2. Buy them from another player who researched them

Check resources like **Allakhazam** or the **P1999 wiki** for which spells at your level are research-only vs. vendor-available.

## 💡 Practical Tips

> ⚡ **Don't neglect your spell upgrades.** A level 30 Cleric casting level 20 heals is a liability. Buy spells as soon as you can afford them.

> 💊 **Carry reagents.** Some spells require reagents (ingredients) to cast. Check your spell descriptions — items like Peridot (for mana drain), Bone Chips (Necromancer pets), or Tiny Jade Inlaid Box (certain research spells) must be in your inventory.

> 🔄 **Swap spell loadouts for different situations.** Most veteran casters carry multiple spell "sets":
> - A **solo set** — direct damage, self-buffs, root/snare
> - A **group set** — heals, buffs, crowd control
> - A **travel set** — Spirit of the Wolf, levitate, see invisible

> 📝 **Memorize your most-used spells permanently.** Put heal, gate, and your best nuke in gem slots you never swap. Build a habit around muscle memory.

> 💰 **Sell or trade vendor-bought spells** you don't need to other players. A Necromancer can buy Wizard spells and resell them — there's an informal economy around this on classic servers.
