# 🌍 Getting Started in EverQuest — A Noob's Guide

Welcome to Norrath! EverQuest is one of the most rewarding MMORPGs ever made, but it can be brutal and confusing at first. This guide will help you survive your first steps. Read everything — this game does not hold your hand.

---

## 🖥️ Where to Play

### EverQuest Live (EQLive)
The official, still-active EverQuest game maintained by Daybreak Game Company.

- **Free to Play:** You can play for free up to **level 85** with All Access membership unlocking the full game and all expansions.
- **Download:** Get the client from the official site at [everquest.com](https://www.everquest.com) — use the Daybreak launcher.
- **Getting Started:**
  1. Create a free Daybreak account at daybreakgames.com
  2. Download and install the Daybreak Game Launcher
  3. Install EverQuest through the launcher
  4. Log in and create your character
- **Current State:** EQLive has 30+ years of expansions. The world is enormous. Many zones have been updated with modern quality-of-life features like the Mercenary system (hireable NPC companions), which makes solo play much more viable for new players.
- **Servers:** EQLive has regular servers, True Box progression servers (relive older eras), and special seasonal servers. For a brand-new player, a **standard server** like Bristlebane or Cazic-Thule is fine to start.

---

### Project 1999 (P1999)
A **free, fan-run emulated server** that recreates the original "classic" EverQuest experience — no modern conveniences, no mercenaries, no hand-holding. This is EverQuest as it was from 1999 through the Velious expansion (~2001).

- **Cost:** Completely free to play.
- **Community:** One of the best, most active classic EQ communities online.
- **Getting Started:**
  1. Go to [project1999.com](https://www.project1999.com)
  2. Register a free forum account — your forum login IS your game login
  3. Download the P1999 client files (instructions are on the wiki/forums)
  4. You'll need an old EverQuest Titanium client OR follow the guide on the P1999 wiki to obtain the necessary client files legally
  5. Patch using the provided patcher
  6. Log in and pick a server (see below)

#### P1999 Green vs Blue Servers

| | **Green Server** | **Blue Server** |
|---|---|---|
| **Era** | Progression — started at Classic, now in Velious | Fully unlocked through Velious (all content open) |
| **Population** | Higher — more active, newer players | Moderate — experienced players, more twinks |
| **Feel** | Fresh, competitive, community events | Established, relaxed, lots of helpful veterans |
| **Recommendation** | Best for new players wanting the full journey | Good if you want help from experienced players |

**Green** is generally recommended for new players since the community is larger and more active. Races and classes are the same on both servers.

> **Note:** P1999 is a strict classic recreation. There is no tutorial, no Overseer system, no mercenaries, and the world is intentionally dangerous. Death is punishing. The experience is incredibly rewarding.

## 🧙 Character Creation

### Server Selection for Noobs
- **EQLive:** Start on a standard server. Avoid brand-new progression servers until you understand the game — they're competitive and fast-paced.
- **P1999:** Start on **Green** server for the most active population and community events.

### Race and Class Selection
EverQuest has a deep, interlocking race/class system that meaningfully impacts your play experience. This is not a cosmetic choice.

For detailed breakdowns of every race and class combination, stats, and playstyle recommendations, see:
👉 **[classes-and-races.md](./classes-and-races.md)**

**Quick tips:**
- **Beginner-friendly classes:** Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight (tanky, straightforward), Ranger, Druid (utility + survival), Necromancer (solo-friendly pets)
- **Hard mode for noobs:** Enchanter (incredibly powerful but complex), Monk (gear-dependent, punishing), Bard (requires active "twisting" of songs)
- **Race matters:** Each race starts in a different city, has different stat bonuses, and has different faction relationships. Trolls and Ogres make excellent warriors but start in evil cities. Wood Elves make great rangers but are fragile.
- **Avoid stranding yourself:** Evil races (Iksar, Troll, Ogre, Dark Elf) are KOS (Kill on Sight) in most good-aligned cities. This limits where you can safely shop and travel as a new player.

### Naming Your Character
- Names must be lore-appropriate — no "xXDarkSlayerXx" or "Pwnmaster"
- Names are **permanent** and unique per server
- Keep it fantasy-sounding. GMs can force a rename for inappropriate names.
- Good practice: name your character something that fits your race/class (e.g., a High Elf cleric named "Aelindra" feels right; "Killbot" does not)

### Starting Stats Explained

Every character has six base stats. These are modified by your race and class, and you get a small pool of points to distribute at creation.

| Stat | Abbreviation | What It Does |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **Strength** | STR | Increases melee damage, carry weight capacity |
| **Stamina** | STA | Increases total hit points (HP) — critical for survival |
| **Agility** | AGI | Improves armor class (AC) and avoidance |
| **Dexterity** | DEX | Affects proc rates (weapon special effects), attack speed benefits |
| **Wisdom** | WIS | Mana pool for Priests (Clerics, Druids, Shamans) — put points here if you're a WIS caster |
| **Intelligence** | INT | Mana pool for Mage/INT casters (Wizards, Mages, Enchanters, Necromancers) |
| **Charisma** | CHA | Affects merchant prices, some NPC faction reactions |

**New player stat tips:**
- If you're a melee class, dump extra points into **STA** first (more HP = longer survival), then **STR**
- If you're a WIS caster (Cleric, Druid, Shaman), max **WIS** for more mana
- If you're an INT caster (Wizard, Mage, Necromancer, Enchanter), max **INT**
- **AGI** is underrated — it quietly helps your AC more than it looks
- Don't overthink it. At low levels the differences are small. Just avoid dumping points into stats irrelevant to your class.

## 🎮 The UI and Controls

EverQuest's interface was designed in 1999. It's not intuitive by modern standards, but it's powerful once you learn it.

### Movement
- **W / Up Arrow** — Move forward
- **S / Down Arrow** — Move backward
- **A / Left Arrow** — Turn left (not strafe by default)
- **D / Right Arrow** — Turn right (not strafe by default)
- **Page Up** — Look up (useful for flying mobs, cliffs)
- **Page Down** — Look down
- **Home** — Auto-run (toggle)
- **Right-click + drag mouse** — Rotate your camera view
- **Numpad** — Alternate movement controls on some setups

> **Tip:** You can enable mouselook by pressing **Insert** or right-clicking and holding to look around freely. Many veterans remap keys so A/D strafe instead of turn.

### Combat — Auto-Attack
- Press **A** to toggle auto-attack on/off (you'll see your character begin swinging)
- You must be facing and close to a target
- Left-click a mob to target it, then press A
- Melee combat is largely automatic — your character swings on a timer
- **Never run away from a mob while it's hitting you** — EQ has a "flee" mechanic where fleeing mobs get a free hit and can backstab you for massive damage

### Spell Bar and Memorizing Spells
- Spellcasting classes have **8 spell gem slots** at the bottom of the screen
- You must **memorize** spells into these slots before you can cast them
- To memorize: open your **Spell Book** (the book icon or press **B**), then drag a spell onto a gem slot
- Memorizing takes time — you must sit still while spells "load"
- You can only have 8 spells ready at once — managing which spells you have memorized is a core skill
- To cast: click the gem, or set it to a hotkey
- **Spellcasting is interrupted** if you take damage — move away from enemies before casting if possible, or have a tank hold aggro

### Inventory Management
- Press **I** to open your inventory
- You have a main inventory (worn slots + bags) and a cursor slot
- **Bags** significantly expand storage — always equip bags in your inventory slots ASAP. Buy them from vendors or loot them early.
- **Worn slots:** head, chest, arms, wrists (x2), hands, legs, feet, neck, ears (x2), fingers (x2), back, waist, range, ammo
- Right-click items for info and options
- Items on your **cursor** are "in hand" — if you log out with something on your cursor it may be lost. Always put items in your bags.

### Chat Windows and Channels

| Command | Channel | Who Hears It |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| `/say <message>` | Say | Players and NPCs near you |
| `/tell <name> <message>` | Tell | Private message to one player |
| `/ooc <message>` | Out of Character | Everyone in your current zone |
| `/shout <message>` | Shout | Everyone in your current zone (use sparingly) |
| `/group <message>` | Group | Your party members only |
| `/gu <message>` | Guild | Your guild members |
| `/auction <message>` | Auction | Everyone in zone — used for buying/selling |

> **Etiquette:** Don't spam `/shout`. Use `/ooc` for general zone chat. Save `/shout` for emergencies or genuinely zone-wide announcements.

### /who and /who all
- `/who` — Lists players in your current zone
- `/who all` — Lists all players on the server (limited display)
- `/who all <name>` — Search for a specific player by name
- `/who all <class>` — Find all players of a class (e.g., `/who all cleric`)
- This is invaluable for finding help, checking who's in your zone, or locating friends.

### Hotbars and Hotkeys
- EQ has multiple **hotbars** — rows of buttons you can assign spells, abilities, and macros to
- Right-click a hotbar button to assign a spell, ability, or create a custom macro
- **Macros** are powerful — you can make a button that says something in chat AND performs an action
- Default hotkeys: **F1–F12** target group members, number keys activate hotbar slots
- You can open additional hotbars through the Options menu — experienced players often run 3-4 hotbars

## 🗺️ Your First Hour

### Talk to Your Guild Master
When you first appear in the world, find your **Guild Master** NPC near your starting point. Guild Masters give **newbie quests** — simple tasks that reward basic armor, weapons, and experience. Do these first. The gear is humble but better than nothing, and the quests teach you how the game works.

### Don't Leave Your Starting Area Too Quickly
EverQuest's world is vast and punishing. Your starting zone is designed to be survivable at level 1-5. **Do not wander into the next zone** until you are comfortable. Many adjacent zones have mobs that will one-shot a new character.

- Stay near the city until level 5-8
- Learn where guards are — guards protect you if you're being chased (run to them!)
- Learn your zone layout — use `/loc` to see your X, Y coordinates

### How to Loot Corpses
When you kill a mob, it leaves a **corpse** with loot:
- Left-click the corpse to open the loot window
- Click individual items to loot them, or use **Loot All** to grab everything
- Always check corpses — even "trash" drops can be sold for coin
- Corpses disappear after a while — don't wait too long to loot

### Selling to Vendors
- Find a merchant NPC (usually inside cities near the guild areas)
- Left-click the merchant to open the trade window
- Drag items from your inventory to the sell area
- Sell junk loot constantly — your carry weight is limited and coin adds up
- **Haggling:** Your **CHA** stat slightly affects buy/sell prices. Higher CHA = slightly better prices. Don't stress about this early on.

### Asking for Buffs (and the Ancient Art of /beg)
- EverQuest has a **/beg** skill — yes, begging is literally a skill Beggars can train. It does almost nothing useful. Do not invest in it. It is mostly a joke and a nod to classic MMO culture.
- **Asking for buffs politely** is a time-honored EQ tradition:
  - Type in `/ooc` or `/say`: *"Any druids or shamans willing to throw some buffs on a new player? =)"*
  - Say **please** and **thank you** — the community responds warmly to politeness
  - Common buffs to ask for: **Strength of Earth** (shaman STA buff), **Spirit of Wolf** (SoW — movement speed, game-changing), **Skin like Wood / Barkskin** (AC buff from druids), **Symbol of X** (Cleric HP buff)
  - **Spirit of Wolf (SoW)** from a Druid or Shaman is arguably the most valuable buff in the game for a new player — it dramatically increases movement speed and survival
  - Always thank the caster — buffs cost them mana and time

### 🔗 Binding Your Soul (/bind)
**This is critically important.** When you die, you respawn at your **bind point**. By default you are bound at your race's starting city — which may be a continent away from where you're hunting.

#### How to Bind
- You **cannot** bind yourself. You need another player (or an NPC on EQLive) to cast **Bind Affinity** on you.
- Classes that can bind others: **Cleric, Druid, Wizard, Enchanter** (and Necromancers can self-bind)
- Ask in `/ooc`: *"Any Druids/Clerics/Wizards able to bind me please?"*
- Stand in the spot you want to be bound — a city, an inn, or a safe outdoor area near your hunting zone

#### Where to Bind
- **Near where you're hunting** — so a death means a short run back, not a cross-continent trek
- **Inside a city or inn** — safe, easy to reach, good for general use
- **At a zone line** close to a dungeon — minimizes corpse run distance
- Avoid binding in dangerous zones where getting back after death would require fighting through the same mobs that killed you

#### Checking Your Bind Point
There's no `/showbind` command — you only discover your bind point when you die. Prevent surprises by always asking for a re-bind when you move to a new area.

> **Rule of thumb:** Every time you travel to a new hunting area and plan to stay, get re-bound there.

### 🚪 Returning to Your Bind Point: The Gate Spell

**Gate** is a spell that **instantly teleports you back to your bind point**, no matter where you are in the world. It is one of the most important spells in EverQuest.

#### Who Gets Gate
| Class | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Druid** | Gets Gate early (level 14); also has group ports |
| **Wizard** | Gets Gate; also has long-distance teleport spells |
| **Necromancer** | Gets Gate; can also use it as a panic escape |
| **Cleric** | Gets Gate; lifesaving in emergencies |
| **Enchanter** | Gets Gate |
| **Shadowknight** | Gets Gate at higher levels |
| **Paladin** | Gets Gate at higher levels |
| **Magician** | Gets Gate |
| **Shaman** | Does **not** get Gate — bind point matters even more |
| **Warrior, Monk, Rogue, Ranger, Bard, Beastlord, Berserker** | No Gate — rely on Druid/Wizard ports or running |

#### Using Gate
1. Memorize Gate in one of your 8 spell slots
2. Stand still and cast it (takes a few seconds — do not move!)
3. You will be teleported instantly to your bind point

#### Gate as an Emergency Escape
- If a fight goes badly and you're about to die, **Gate out immediately**
- Gate takes ~3–4 seconds to cast — start casting **before** you're at critically low health
- Gate is cancelled if you take damage during the cast on some server settings (P1999: Gate is interruptible; EQLive: less so)
- **Hotbar Gate** — put it on an easy-to-reach hotkey so you can hit it in a panic

> **Tip:** Many experienced players keep Gate in spell slot 8 (their last slot) and have a hotkey always ready. When something goes wrong, muscle memory takes over.

#### Classes Without Gate: Your Options
If you play a class without Gate (Warrior, Monk, Rogue, etc.):
- **Stay close to zone lines** when hunting — ducking into the next zone drops aggro
- **Ask a Druid or Wizard for a port** back to your home city (tip them plat!)
- **Feign Death** (Monks, Necromancers, Shadowknights) — drops mob aggro, lets you escape
- Plan your hunting spots with escape routes in mind

## 💀 Death and Corpse Runs

EverQuest death is not a slap on the wrist. It is a core mechanic that shapes how you play.

### What Happens When You Die
1. Your character **drops a corpse** — your corpse holds **all your equipped gear and inventory**
2. You respawn naked (or in your underwear) at your **bind point**
3. You lose a percentage of your **current experience** — you can even **de-level** if you die repeatedly at the edge of a level (this is brutal and real)
4. Your bind point might be far, far away

### How to Do a Corpse Run
1. Respawn naked at your bind point
2. Navigate back to where you died — usually dangerous, since you have no gear
3. Locate your corpse — it glows and has your name
4. **Loot your corpse** to recover your items
5. If the area is too dangerous to return to safely:
   - Ask for help in `/ooc` — experienced players often help with corpse runs
   - On P1999, ask in the **Newbie** channel if available
   - Find a cleric who can **Summon Corpse** (brings your corpse to you — incredible spell)
   - Wait near a zone boundary for help

### Tips for Surviving Corpse Runs
- **Stay close to zone lines** when hunting dangerous areas — you can duck out if things go wrong
- **Never go AFK** in an unsafe location
- Keep a set of **cheap backup gear** in the bank if you're in a risky area
- The fear of death in EQ changes how you play — it makes you careful, deliberate, and attentive

### Graveyards (EQLive)
On EQLive, many zones have **Graveyards** — when you die in a zone with a graveyard, your corpse may be moved there after a timer. This is a modern quality-of-life feature that makes corpse recovery less punishing. Check the zone map for the graveyard icon.

### Soulbinding — Why It Matters
Your bind point determines everything about death recovery. A bad bind (e.g., bound in your racial city on the opposite side of the continent from where you're hunting) means:
- A potentially 30+ minute naked run back to your corpse
- Running through dangerous zones with no gear
- Possible additional deaths trying to get back

**Always keep your bind point updated** as you move to new hunting areas.

### Getting a Cleric Rez
A Cleric's **Resurrection** spell is the most powerful death mitigation in the game:
- A full rez (96% rez) **restores all lost experience** from that death
- Lower-level rez spells restore partial experience
- Clerics are the most beloved class in the game partly because of this
- When asking for a rez: `/ooc` or `/tell` a cleric, explain where your corpse is, and be patient and grateful
- **Always tip or offer to help in return** — a rez is a significant gift of a cleric's time

## 🤝 Social Etiquette

EverQuest has a strong social culture built over decades. The community largely self-polices based on shared norms. Learn these early — your reputation on a server is persistent and matters.

### Camping (Claiming a Spot)
- A **camp** is a specific spawn location you're farming — a named mob, a chest, a vendor area
- To claim a camp: politely announce in `/ooc`: *"Taking camp at [location], first here?"*
- If someone says they're already camping it, **honor the claim** — this is EQ law
- Camps have an **implied queue** — if someone is camping a spawn and you ask, they may let you "second camp" (you're next when they leave)
- **Kill stealing (KSing)** — attacking or looting a mob that another player is already fighting — is a serious breach of etiquette and will earn you a terrible reputation
- **Do not train someone out of their camp** (see below)

### Trains
A **train** is what happens when a player accidentally (or maliciously) pulls a long chain of mobs behind them and runs through another player's camp or zone.

- Trains happen to everyone eventually — don't be too hard on someone who accidentally trains you
- If YOU are creating a train: **yell "TRAIN TO ZONE"** (`/shout Train to zone!`) immediately to warn others
- Run to the zone exit to "zone out" and lose the train — don't run through other players' camps
- **Malicious training** (intentionally pulling mobs onto other players) is a bannable offense and will result in ostracism from the community

### Asking for Groups
EQ is a group-focused game. Finding a group is part of the adventure.

- Use `/ooc [zone]` to advertise yourself: *"LFG — Level 12 Warrior, can tank or assist, looking for group in Blackburrow"*
- **LFG** = Looking for Group
- Be specific: mention your level, class, and location
- If you join a group, **ask what they're doing, where they're pulling, and what the kill order is** before diving in
- Be on time, be communicative, and **don't go AFK without telling the group**
- When the group disbands, always say "thanks for the group!" — it goes a long way

### Buffs — Giving and Receiving
- If a high-level player buffs you without you asking, they're being kind — **always say thank you**
- If you ask for buffs and someone declines, thank them anyway — they owe you nothing
- Don't demand buffs. Don't harass players for buffs. One polite ask is enough.
- Offering a small tip of coin for buffs is a gesture of appreciation (especially on P1999 where even a few gold means something)

### General Respect
- EQ has a **persistent world and persistent reputation** — especially on smaller servers like P1999
- Be kind, helpful, and patient. The community will remember you.
- Help new players when you can — even pointing someone in the right direction with a `/tell` makes a difference
- The player who helped you today might be the guild leader who invites you in six months
