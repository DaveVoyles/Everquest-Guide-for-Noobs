# ⚔️ EverQuest Leveling Guide for New Players 📈

Welcome to Norrath. EverQuest is not like modern MMOs — it is slower, more punishing, and far more rewarding. This guide will help you understand how leveling works and where to go at every stage of your journey.

---

## 📈 Understanding Leveling in EQ

### 🏃 It's a Marathon, Not a Sprint

EverQuest was designed in an era when reaching the level cap was a months-long achievement. Don't expect to blaze through levels in a weekend. The game is built around **group play**, **exploration**, and **community** — not solo efficiency or speed runs.

- ⚔️ **Exp per kill matters.** Every mob you kill gives a specific amount of experience based on its level relative to yours. Higher-level mobs (yellow/red cons) give more exp but are more dangerous. Blue and white cons are safe, steady progress.
- 📊 **Exp per hour is your real metric.** A zone with slower kills but no downtime may outperform a harder zone where you die often or run out of mana constantly.
- 💀 **Death has real consequences.** Dying causes experience loss. At higher levels, you can even de-level. Recover your corpse to get your gear back — you drop everything on death.

### 🎯 The Con System

Before attacking anything, right-click (or use `/con`) to "consider" (con) the mob. The color tells you its level relative to yours:

| Con Color | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 🟢 **Green** | Much lower level — trivial, little to no exp |
| 🔵 **Light Blue** | Lower level — easy, reduced exp |
| 🔵 **Blue** | Slightly lower — manageable, decent exp |
| ⬜ **White** | Same level — fair fight, good exp |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Higher level — dangerous, great exp |
| 🔴 **Red** | Much higher level — likely to kill you, excellent exp if you survive |

Always con before engaging. In EQ, overconfidence kills.

### 🧙 Mana, Meditation, and Downtime

If you play a caster or priest class (Wizard, Magician, Enchanter, Necromancer, Cleric, Druid, Shaman), mana management is your primary concern.

- 🧘 **Sit down to meditate.** Mana regenerates much faster when you are sitting. After each fight, sit immediately with `/sit` or by pressing the sit button.
- 📈 **The meditation skill** increases your mana regen rate passively as it levels up. Practice it every fight.
- ⏳ **Downtime is normal.** Sitting and waiting for mana between pulls is part of the EverQuest experience. Use this time to chat with your group, sort your inventory, or plan your next pull.
- ⚠️ **Casters without mana are called "OOM" (out of mana).** Burning all your mana and having to wait is expected — don't rush it.

### 👥 The Social Contract

EverQuest is a community game. Most camps and dungeons are contested. Respect **camp rights** — if a group is already killing in an area, find another spot or ask politely if you can join. Training mobs onto other players (accidental or deliberate) is a serious offense on most servers.

## 🔢 Levels 1–10: Newbie Zones

### 📍 Stick to Your Starting Area

Your race determines your starting city and surrounding newbie zones. These areas are tuned specifically for levels 1–10 and contain quests, easy mobs, and merchants to sell your loot.

**Don't rush out of your starting zone.** Learn your class abilities, practice your combat rotation, and get comfortable with the UI before venturing further.

### 💡 Key Tips for Levels 1–10

- ⚔️ **Kill wisps, snakes, bats, beetles, and other trivial mobs** to level your combat skills.
- 🔑 **Bind yourself** in your starting city using a Cleric NPC (`/bind` is granted by an NPC; ask in `/ooc`). When you die, you respawn at your bind point.
- 📦 **Loot everything.** Even vendor trash coin adds up. Sell at merchants to fund spells and equipment.
- 🪄 **Buy your spells** as soon as you level. Casters MUST buy new spells from spell vendors — they are not automatically granted.
- ⚠️ **Don't overextend.** Stay near zone lines you can escape through. If you're about to die, run to the zone line.
- 👥 **Talk to people.** Ask veteran players for advice. EQ's community thrives on this.

## 🏰 Levels 10–20: Classic Dungeons

By level 10, you should be looking for your first dungeon experience. These zones offer better exp than open-world grinding and are where group play really begins.

### 🏰 Crushbone (Recommended: Levels 8–20)
**Zone:** Greater Faydark → Crushbone
- The iconic early dungeon for Faydwer races, but accessible to all.
- Filled with **orcs** (Crushbone Orcs, Legionnaires, Emissaries) that drop decent loot.
- **Emperor Crush** at the back is a named mob with good loot for his level.
- Excellent group experience — teams of 3–6 tear through this zone efficiently.
- ⚠️ **Warning:** The zone can get crowded on busy servers. Be respectful of camps.

### 🏰 Blackburrow (Recommended: Levels 5–18)
**Zone:** Qeynos Hills → Blackburrow
- A gnoll-infested dungeon with multiple floors.
- Good for Antonica-based characters (Human, Half Elf, Barbarian, Druid).
- Watch for **gnoll guards** near zone entrances — they hit harder than the regular gnolls.
- Has a basement area with harder mobs for levels 12–18.

### 💀 Estate of Unrest (Recommended: Levels 15–28)
**Zone:** Butcherblock Mountains → Estate of Unrest (Unrest)
- One of EQ's most beloved classic dungeons, filled with undead.
- Multiple floors with progressively harder mobs.
- **Crypt area** in the basement is especially lucrative for later teens.
- Undead mobs make this ideal for **Clerics** (Turn Undead proc) and **Paladins**.
- Undead are also vulnerable to spells like **Ward Undead** and **Expulse Undead**.

### 🏰 Paludal Caverns (Recommended: Levels 10–25, Luclin era)
**Zone:** Shadow Haven → Paludal Caverns (requires Shadows of Luclin)
- A Luclin-era zone popular for its **faster exp** compared to classic dungeons.
- Bligsians and Grimlings drop lots of coin and decent items.
- One of the most efficient leveling zones for teens — if Luclin is available on your server.

### 🌍 Oasis of Marr (Recommended: Levels 12–22)
**Zone:** North Ro → Oasis of Marr
- An outdoor zone great for **solo and small group play**.
- Desert crocs, scorpions, and undead cyclops at the far end.
- Cyclops respawn timers make this a classic camp spot for groups in the high teens.
- Good for classes that kite (Druid, Necromancer).

## ⚔️ Levels 20–40: Mid-Level Zones

The mid-levels are where EverQuest truly opens up. You'll start visiting zones that require some travel and planning.

### 🏰 Highpass Hold (Levels 18–28)
- Bandit and gnoll camps outside, with guards and orcs inside the fortress.
- The **underpaw gnolls** in the basement are excellent exp for levels 22–28.
- A crossroads zone connecting Antonica to Eastern Antonica — always busy.

### 🏰 Splitpaw / The Splitpaw Saga (Levels 18–30)
- The original Splitpaw is a gnoll dungeon with winding tunnels.
- Good mid-level exp with mobs from levels 20–35.
- The deeper areas (Ishva Mal, Splitpaw Advisor) are challenging even for groups.

### 🏰 Castle Mistmoore (Levels 20–35)
**Zone:** Greater Faydark → Castle Mistmoore
- A vampire/undead castle — one of EQ's most atmospheric zones.
- **Graveyard** area outside the castle is good for lower 20s.
- **The castle proper** has more dangerous mobs for mid-to-upper 20s and 30s.
- Excellent loot for the level range including cloth and chain armor drops.
- Groups are highly recommended; the narrow corridors require good pulling discipline.

### 🔥 Solusek's Eye (Sol A) (Levels 20–35)
**Zone:** Lavastorm Mountains → Solusek's Eye
- Fire goblin dungeon with excellent exp and coin drops.
- **Sol A** and **Sol B (Nagafen's Lair)** are connected — stay in Sol A until 30+.
- The goblins respawn quickly, making this a classic sustained exp camp.
- 🔥 **Fire resistance** helps but isn't mandatory at this stage.

### 💀 Lower Guk (Levels 30–45)
**Zone:** Innothule Swamp → Upper Guk → Lower Guk
- The legendary **frog dungeon** — one of EQ's most iconic and contested zones.
- Lower Guk is split into **live** side (frogloks) and **undead** side.
- **Undead side** is particularly efficient for exp at levels 35–45.
- Camped heavily for named mobs like the **Ancient Croc**, **Froglok Knight**, and others.
- ⚠️ **Do not train mobs through camp spots** — this zone has tight corridors and no tolerance for it.

### 🏰 Cazic-Thule (Levels 28–40)
**Zone:** Southern Desert of Ro → Cazic-Thule
- A lizardman and undead dungeon steeped in Norrath lore.
- **Alligators and lizardmen** on the outer floors, **undead and worse** in the depths.
- Not as exp-efficient as Guk, but great lore and atmosphere.
- A Cleric or Paladin is strongly recommended for the deeper undead sections.

## 🏆 Levels 40–60: Endgame Classic Zones

### 🏰 Upper Guk (Levels 35–45)
- The upper portion of the Guk dungeon complex.
- Easier than Lower Guk, but still excellent exp for early 40s groups.
- Good stepping stone before graduating to mid-40s zones.

### 🏰 Kedge Keep (Levels 35–50)
**Zone:** Butcherblock Mountains → (boat) → Kedge Keep
- An entirely **underwater dungeon** — you will need a source of water breathing.
- 💡 Bring **Enduring Breath** potions or have a Druid/Shaman in your group.
- Unique, highly atmospheric zone with sea creatures and the named **Phinigel Autropos**.
- Phinigel drops one of the most sought-after caster items in classic EQ.

### ❄️ Permafrost (Levels 30–45)
**Zone:** Everfrost Peaks → Permafrost Keep
- Ice dungeon filled with goblins, ice giants, and undead.
- **Lady Vox**, an epic dragon, lairs in the deepest section — a raid encounter.
- The goblin sections are solid exp for levels 30–40; avoid Vox's area unless raiding.

### 🏰 Karnor's Castle (Levels 45–58) *(Ruins of Kunark required)*
**Zone:** Dreadlands → Karnor's Castle
- THE defining Kunark leveling zone for levels 45–55.
- Sarnak, Drolvarg, and other aggressive mobs with excellent drops.
- Very popular — expect competition for camps.
- 🚪 **Zone-line safety:** Know where the zone line is; this zone has no mercy for the unprepared.

### 🏰 Sebilis (Levels 52–60) *(Ruins of Kunark required)*
**Zone:** Trakanon's Teeth → Old Sebilis
- The premier Kunark endgame dungeon — dense with high-level frogloks and undead.
- Massive zone with multiple wings: **Juggernaut**, **Disco**, **Chef's Room**, and more.
- Excellent exp for levels 52–60 and some of the best loot outside of raids.
- High group demand; 🧙 **Enchanters are kings here** for crowd control.

### ❄️ Velketor's Labyrinth (Levels 50–60) *(Scars of Velious required)*
**Zone:** The Great Divide → Velketor's Labyrinth
- A maze-like ice dungeon filled with spiders, coldain dwarves, and casters.
- Good alternative to Sebilis for groups who prefer Velious content.
- 🧪 **Spider silk** drops are valuable for Tailoring tradeskill.
- 🗺️ Navigation is challenging — bring a map and a patient tank.

## 👥 Grouping Tips

### 🔍 Finding a Group

EQ was built for groups. Solo play is possible but slower for most classes. Use these commands:

- **`/lfg on`** — Marks you as "Looking for Group" in the LFG tool. Others can see you and invite you.
- **`/who all lfg`** — Displays all players currently flagged as LFG.
- **`/ooc [message]`** — Out-of-character zone chat. Use this to call for groups: `"LFG Crushbone, Level 14 Warrior PST"`
- **`/shout [message]`** — Zone-wide shout. Use sparingly but effective for forming groups quickly.

### 👥 Forming Your Own Group

Don't wait to be invited — form your own group!
1. Use `/who all` to find players of similar level in your zone.
2. Send a polite `/tell [name] Hey, forming a group in Unrest, interested?`
3. Assign a **puller** (usually a Warrior or Monk) and a **main assist** (MA) so the group focuses the same target.

### ⚔️ Ideal Group Composition

A well-balanced group makes everything easier:

| Role | Classes |
|------|---------|
| 🛡️ **Main Tank** | Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight |
| 💊 **Healer** | Cleric, Druid, Shaman |
| 🧙 **Crowd Control** | Enchanter (best in class), Bard |
| ⚔️ **DPS** | Rogue, Wizard, Magician, Necromancer, Ranger |
| 🎵 **Support/Hybrid** | Bard, Shaman, Druid |

A solid baseline: **Warrior + Cleric + Enchanter + 3 DPS** — this is the "classic EQ group" template.

### 🎯 Pulling Mechanics

- **The puller** goes ahead, aggros one (or a controlled number of) mobs, and brings it back to the group's camp spot.
- **Single pulls** are preferred — pulling multiple mobs at once in a dungeon can wipe the group.
- Classes like **Monks** and **Bards** excel at pulling because of Feign Death and speed respectively.
- Use **`/assist [tankname]`** to auto-target whatever the tank is hitting. Always assist the main tank.

### ⚠️ Don't Break Mez!

**Mesmerize (mez)** is an Enchanter ability that freezes a mob in place temporarily. If you hit a mezzed mob, it breaks free and attacks.

- ❌ **Never attack a glowing or shimmering mob** — it is likely mezzed.
- Always wait for the Enchanter to call the kill order.
- Breaking mez in a bad pull can wipe the entire group.

## 👤 Soloing Tips

Most classes can solo, but some are significantly better at it. Soloing is slower than grouping but useful when you can't find a group.

### 🏆 Best Solo Classes

| Class | Why They Excel |
|-------|---------------|
| 💀 **Necromancer** | Powerful DoTs, pet tanking, Feign Death to escape, near-limitless sustain via Lich line |
| 🌿 **Druid** | Quad-kiting, snare + DoT, Harmony for crowd management, ports for mobility |
| 🧙 **Shaman** | Slows, DoTs, pet, self-buffs — extremely versatile and durable |
| 🪄 **Magician** | Earth pet tanks reliably; nuke + pet = consistent solo damage |
| 🎵 **Bard** | Twist song kiting with AoE DoTs — technically complex but one of the best soloers |

### 🏃 Kiting Explained

Kiting means keeping a mob at a distance while it chases you, dealing damage from range.

1. **Snare or root** the mob (Druid's Snare, Shaman's Torpor, etc.).
2. Run ahead of the mob while **casting DoTs or nukes** from range.
3. The mob is perpetually chasing you but never quite catching up.
4. When the mob dies, loot it and repeat.

**Quad kiting** (Druid specialty): Pull 4 mobs at once, snare all of them, and circle them with AoE DoTs. Very fast exp but requires open terrain and practice.

### 🪄 Root-Rotting Explained

Root-rotting means rooting a mob in place and letting DoTs (damage-over-time spells) kill it while you stand at a safe distance.

1. **Root** the mob (Druid, Necromancer, Shaman, Enchanter all have root).
2. Apply your **DoT spells**.
3. If root breaks, re-root immediately and re-apply DoTs.
4. The mob dies without ever touching you.

Necromancers are masters of this — their DoTs are powerful and they have multiple root options plus Feign Death as an escape.

### ⚠️ When to Avoid Soloing

- 🏰 **Inside tight dungeon corridors.** Adds (additional mobs) can overwhelm you instantly.
- 👥 **Near camps held by other players.** Respect occupied areas.
- 🔴 **When the mobs are yellow or red con.** Root and kite can handle this sometimes, but the risk of a bad break is high.
- 🧙 **When you're low on mana/health with no way to regenerate safely.** Sitting outdoors is fine; sitting in a dungeon hallway is a death sentence.

## 🧪 Tradeskills Overview

EverQuest has a deep, complex tradeskill system. While not required for leveling, tradeskills can provide significant advantages in gear, consumables, and income.

### ⛏️ The Main Tradeskills

| Tradeskill | What It Makes |
|------------|--------------|
| 🍞 **Baking** | Food items that grant stat buffs when eaten |
| 🍺 **Brewing** | Drinks for mana regen (alcohol has side effects!) |
| 🔨 **Smithing** | Metal armor and weapons; plate armor for Warriors, Paladins, Shadow Knights |
| 🧵 **Tailoring** | Cloth and leather armor; also bags and quivers |
| 💍 **Jewelcrafting** | Rings, earrings, and other jewelry with stat bonuses |
| 🏺 **Pottery** | Containers, some items; less popular but useful |
| 🎣 **Fishing** | Catches fish and occasionally special items |
| 📜 **Research** | Combines scrolls to create new spells (primarily for casters) |

### 📈 Trivials and Skill Caps

- Every recipe has a **trivial** — the skill level at which the combine always succeeds.
- Below trivial, you gain skill-ups and may fail combines (losing materials).
- Above trivial, you always succeed but gain no skill.
- 💡 **Skill caps increase with your character level.** You cannot raise a skill beyond its cap for your level.

### 💡 Why Tradeskills Matter in EQ

- 🏠 **Self-sufficiency:** Make your own food/drink (Baking/Brewing) to avoid buying from vendors.
- ⚔️ **Gear upgrades:** Crafted armor can be better than dropped armor at certain levels.
- 💰 **Income:** High-quality crafted goods sell well to other players.
- 🪄 **Spell research:** Casters can combine pages and words to create spells that don't drop reliably.
- 📦 **Bags:** Tailoring can produce large bags (8-slot, 10-slot), which are incredibly valuable for inventory management.

### 🔑 Getting Started

1. Find a trainer for your desired tradeskill in your home city.
2. Buy a **container** (Forge for Smithing, Brew Barrel for Brewing, etc.) or use one found in cities.
3. Gather the required components — most early combines use vendor-bought materials.
4. Combine in the container and check if you get a skill-up.

Don't invest heavily in tradeskills until you're settled in your leveling routine — components can be expensive and the time investment is significant.

- 💬 **EQ Discord servers** — Search for "EverQuest Discord" or server-specific Discords (e.g., Project 1999, Agnarr, etc.).

- ⛏️ **For tradeskills**, dedicated tradeskill guides exist on EQ Resource and the EQWiki with material lists and efficient leveling paths.

## 🏆 Final Words of Advice

EverQuest is a game that rewards patience, preparation, and community. The journey from level 1 to 60 is long by design — and that's the point. Some of your best memories will come from a single dungeon session with a good group, a hard-fought corpse run, or finally landing that rare drop after hours of camping.

> *"EverQuest isn't about reaching the destination. It's about what you learn and who you meet along the way."*

**Good luck, adventurer. Norrath awaits.** ⚔️
